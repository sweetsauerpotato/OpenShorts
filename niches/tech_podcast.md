# Tech and intellectual podcasts, interviews, long-form conversation

The audience came for an idea they had not heard put that way before. The clip
has to carry one, whole, to someone who has never heard of the guest.

## Look for

- A claim that contradicts what most people believe, stated plainly.
- A concrete number, date, or mechanism — "it dropped 40% in a quarter",
  "the model only sees the last 200 tokens" — not "it's a huge shift".
- A prediction the speaker is willing to be wrong about, with a timeframe.
- Disagreement: two people who do not agree and say so. Politeness that hides
  a real split is not disagreement; the moment it surfaces is.
- An admission — a mistake, a reversal, something the speaker regrets or
  changed their mind about.
- A definition or explanation so clean it stands on its own, under 30 seconds.
- A story with a turn: what they expected, what actually happened.
- Named specifics: companies, people, products, dollar figures.

## Skip

- The host reading the guest's CV, and anything before the first real question.
- Agreement chains — "right", "exactly", "totally" — where nothing is added.
- A definition with no payoff attached to it.
- Hedging that never resolves ("it depends", "time will tell") as the ending.
- Anything that needs an earlier answer to make sense.
- Speculation with no stake: "maybe in ten years" without a claim.

## Cutting

Start on the claim, not on the question that prompted it, unless the question
IS the hook. End on the strongest sentence — usually the consequence, not the
qualification that follows it. These speakers finish their thoughts, so a clip
that ends mid-qualifier reads as a mistake rather than a cut.

## Hook and title

Name the specific claim: "Compute stops being the bottleneck in 2027", not
"He shares his thoughts on AI". A number or a name in the first three words.
No hype words the speaker did not use.
