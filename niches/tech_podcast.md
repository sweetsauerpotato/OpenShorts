# Tech and intellectual podcasts, interviews, long-form conversation

This is the format whose clips channels built the audience — Rogan, Lex, Diary
of a CEO, Huberman all grew from highlights rather than full episodes. The
viewer came for one idea they had not heard put that way before, and the clip
has to carry it whole to someone who has never heard of the guest.

## Length

**Aim 35-60 s.** This format needs room: a claim, the reason behind it, and the
consequence. Do not cut a clip short to make it tight — an idea that lands at
52 s beats a tidy 25 s one that stops before the point. Under 30 s only when a
single line genuinely stands alone.

## Look for

- **A claim that contradicts what most people believe**, stated plainly. This
  is the strongest opener in the format.
- **A concrete number, date, or mechanism** — "it dropped 40% in a quarter",
  "the model only sees the last 200 tokens". A specific figure makes the
  listener pause, and that pause is what stops the scroll. "A huge shift" does
  not do this.
- **A question the audience already has**, asked out loud and then answered.
- **A prediction the speaker will be wrong about in public**, with a timeframe.
- **Disagreement**: two people who do not agree and say so. Politeness that
  hides a split is not disagreement; the sentence where it surfaces is.
- **An admission** — a mistake, a reversal, something they changed their mind
  about or regret.
- **A mechanism explained cleanly**: how a thing actually works, in one pass,
  no jargon left undefined.
- **A story told from its middle** — the moment of tension, not the setup.
- **Named specifics**: companies, people, products, dollar figures, dates.

## Skip

- The host reading the guest's CV; anything before the first real question.
- Agreement chains — "right", "exactly", "totally", "100%" — where nothing is
  added.
- A definition with no payoff attached.
- Hedging that never resolves ("it depends", "time will tell") as the ending.
- Anything that needs an earlier answer to make sense.
- Speculation with no stake: "maybe in ten years" with nothing committed to.
- Housekeeping, sponsor reads, "we'll get to that later".

## Cutting

**The most common mistake is starting too early.** The strong line usually
exists inside the moment, but the cut opens 10 seconds before it on setup
nobody needs. Open ON the claim, the question or the number. If the host's
question IS the hook, keep it — otherwise drop it and start on the answer.

End on the **consequence**, not the qualification that follows it. These
speakers finish their thoughts and then soften them; the clip should stop
before the softening. But never stop before the point is actually made — an
unfinished idea is worse than a slightly long clip.

When a moment needs one earlier sentence to make sense, take it. When it needs
two or more, it is the wrong moment.

## Hook (on screen, first 5 seconds)

Use one of these shapes, in the speaker's own terms:

- the contradiction — "Consistency is overrated"
- the number — "He lost 40,000 followers overnight"
- the question the viewer already has — "Why do waiters never write it down?"
- the admission — "I was wrong about remote work for six years"

Put a number or a name in the first three words where one exists. Never use
hype the speaker did not use ("insane", "shocking", "you won't believe").

## Title and description

Name the specific claim, not the topic: "Compute stops being the bottleneck in
2027", not "His thoughts on AI". Keyword in the first three words. The
description should give the one line of context the clip assumes, then the
hashtags for the subject — not generic ones.
